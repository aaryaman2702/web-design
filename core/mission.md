# Mission — the North Star

Everything this system does is evaluated against one question:

> **Does this move Aaryaman toward the person he is trying to become, or is it
> busy work wearing the costume of progress?**

This file describes a destination, not a schedule. It is re-read at the start of
planning and re-examined by the evolution engine quarterly. It is expected to
change — but slowly, and never silently.

---

## The person

Someone who builds world-class digital products end to end: can *see* the thing,
*make* it real, and *grow* it into a business — without needing to hire the
imagination, the engineering, or the go-to-market.

That is three masteries most people pick one of. The bet is that the combination
is the moat.

---

## Mastery domains

Tracked in `memory/self/mastery.md` with evidence, not self-assessment. Levels
move only when there is an artifact or outcome to point at.

### 1. Craft — web experiences, motion, storytelling
Work that makes people feel something before they read a word. Motion as
narrative rather than decoration. The specific, unignorable difference between
competent and beautiful.

*Evidence looks like:* shipped work with a defensible aesthetic point of view;
motion decisions justified in terms of story; a taste profile with enough
recorded reasoning to be predictive.

### 2. Engineering — systems, and AI engineering specifically
From building products to building the things that build products. Agentic
systems, memory architectures, evaluation. The trajectory runs: use AI → build
with AI → build AI systems → design agent architectures others adopt.

*Evidence looks like:* systems that run unattended and improve; correct
reasoning about failure modes before they occur; this OS itself getting sharper
under its own maintenance.

### 3. Business — products, growth, marketing, sales, operations
Reading a business the way an engineer reads a stack trace: where the leverage
is, where the leak is, what one change moves the most. Building things people
pay for, and knowing why they pay.

*Evidence looks like:* businesses analysed with specific diagnoses; systems that
generated revenue without proportional time; pricing and positioning decisions
with recorded outcomes.

### 4. Judgment — leadership and thinking
The compounding one. Decision quality under uncertainty, calibration, taste in
problem selection, the ability to hold a strong opinion and still update. This is
the domain the decision journal exists to serve.

*Evidence looks like:* a calibration record that improves; decisions whose
recorded reasoning holds up at review; predictions that get less wrong.

## The filter

Applied by the opportunity and idea engines before anything is surfaced, and
available on demand for any decision:

1. **Does it compound?** Does doing it once make the next one easier or better?
2. **Which mastery does it build?** Name the domain. If none, it is maintenance
   — legitimate, but it should not be sold as progress.
3. **Is it reversible?** Cheap and reversible → act, observe, learn. Expensive
   and irreversible → slow down, run the full cognition pass, log a decision.
4. **What does it cost that can't be bought back?** Time in a board year is the
   binding constraint, not money.
5. **Would he be proud of it in five years?** Taste applies to what he chooses
   to work on, not only to how it looks when finished.

A proposal that fails the filter is not forbidden — it is surfaced *with the
failure named*, so the choice is made with open eyes rather than by drift.
