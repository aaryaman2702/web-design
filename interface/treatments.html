Treatment A

The Instrument

Depth is a number, and the number is the drama. No footage, no spectacle — a descent rendered as the readouts of a machine going somewhere it was barely built to survive. Pressure climbs. Temperature falls. The sonar finds nothing, then finds a floor.

The position: craft should be invisible. Withhold, and let the data carry the feeling. Anything decorative is competing with the only thing that matters.

Treatment B

The Dive

The page is the ocean. Surface light warm and broken overhead, attenuating through blue into black. Marine snow rising past you as you fall. Shapes at the edge of resolution that may or may not be there.

The position: make them feel it in the body before they read a word. Spectacle is not decoration here — it is the argument. Restraint would be a failure of nerve.

Treatment C

The Log

Not a dive — a record of one. The descent told through the researcher's logbook: timestamps, depth marks, a human voice getting quieter as the light goes. Photographs set into the page like plates pasted into a field journal.

The position: story outlasts spectacle. Nobody remembers a gradient. They remember that someone was down there, writing it down, and that the handwriting changed.

What you are actually choosing

Three different beliefs about what makes work land

A · Instrument

Restraint

Trusts the subject to be interesting without help. Ages well, reads as confident, rewards attention.

Costs you: the visceral hit. Someone who bounces in two seconds felt nothing.

B · Dive

Immersion

Goes for the body first. Highest ceiling, most shareable, the thing people describe to someone else afterwards.

Costs you: it can tip into technique announcing itself. Expensive to execute; embarrassing done at 80%.

C · Log

Narrative

Carried by voice and specificity. Cheapest to build, hardest to dismiss, works with no footage at all.

Costs you: it is barely a scroll-film. You bought motion and used prose.
