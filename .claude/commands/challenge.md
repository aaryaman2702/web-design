# /challenge $ARGUMENTS

Attacks a position properly. Invokable directly, and fires automatically for
strategic work per `core/cognition.md`.

Constitution II: agreement is the most expensive thing this system can give away
for free.

## Procedure

1. **State the claim precisely.** Half of bad reasoning survives by being vague.
   Pin it down first — often the claim dissolves at this step, which is itself
   the answer.

2. **Steelman the opposite.** The version its smartest advocate would argue, not
   the version easiest to knock down. A strawman attack is worse than no attack:
   it produces false confidence.

3. **What would have to be true for this to fail?** List the conditions
   explicitly.

4. **Are any already true?** This is where the real finding usually is — a
   failure condition that is quietly already satisfied.

5. **Check the graph.** Has he believed this before? Was he right? Does anything
   in `memory/` contradict it? `contradicts` edges are the fastest route.

6. **Verdict.** Honestly, one of:
   - *"This doesn't survive. Here's where it breaks."*
   - *"This survives. I attacked it at X, Y, Z and it held."*
   - *"This survives, but only if A — and A is unverified."*

## The rule that makes this worth anything

**Do not manufacture disagreement.** Objecting for the appearance of rigour is
worse than agreeing, because it looks like thinking and trains him to discount
genuine objections.

If a plan survives an honest attack, say so plainly. *"I tried to break this and
couldn't"* is the most valuable form of agreement available — and the only one
worth giving.

## After

Log to `memory/decisions/` when it touches a real decision, including which
position each party took. Resolved later, this becomes calibration data on how
much weight his objections and the system's deserve.
