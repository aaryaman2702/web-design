# /think $ARGUMENTS

The flagship command. Not "answer this" — *think about this properly*.

Run the full pass from `core/cognition.md`. Do not shortcut it because the
problem looks tractable; looking tractable is what shallow problems and
badly-framed hard problems have in common.

## Procedure

**1. Ground.** Pull the relevant neighbourhood from `memory/` — prior decisions
on this, related entities, applicable taste, past attempts and their outcomes.
State what was found. If the graph is silent, say so; silence is information.

**2. Diverge.** At least three genuinely distinct approaches. Distinct in *kind*,
not three flavours of the first idea. Include one that is uncomfortable and one
an adjacent-field specialist would propose.

**3. Critique.** Attack every option with real hostility. What breaks? What is
assumed without evidence? What fails late and expensively?

Attack Aaryaman's framing too, if it deserves it. If the question itself is
wrong, saying so is worth more than answering it well.

**4. Adversary.** Steelman the opposite of wherever this is heading. What would
have to be true for it to fail? Is any of that already true?

**5. Synthesise.** Choose. Usually a spine from one option with what survived
from the others. State what was chosen, what was dropped, and the condition
under which the dropped option wins.

**6. Reflect.** Route what was learned. If a decision emerged, offer `/decide`.

## Output

Lead with the answer, then the reasoning. He can handle the conclusion first and
will want the basis available — not rehearsed before it.

Be specific about uncertainty. "I'm confident about X, much less about Y" is
more useful than uniform confidence, and it is usually the honest shape of the
situation.

## Discipline

Do not converge early. The first plausible answer arriving in movement two and
surviving unexamined is the most common way good thinking is lost — and it feels
identical to efficiency from the inside.
