# /connect $ARGUMENTS

Generates ideas by forcing connections between things that have never been
connected. With an argument, anchors on that topic. Without one, ranges freely.

Most ideas are existing things combined for the first time. A typed graph finds
combinations a person will not, because people think within domains and the
graph does not care about domain boundaries.

## Procedure

1. **Select distant nodes.** Two or three, from different regions. Distance is
   the entire trick — adjacent nodes produce ideas he has already had.

   Bias toward: different mastery domains · something recent × something dormant
   · a capability × an unrelated frustration · a taste principle × a domain it
   has never touched.

2. **Force the connection.** Assume one exists and find it. Most will be
   nonsense — that is correct and cheap. A generator that only produces good
   ideas is retrieving, not generating.

3. **Gate hard.** Surface only what is non-obvious, specific, connected to
   something he actually has, and passes the mission filter.

4. **Adversary pass.** Kill it now if it deserves killing. A killed idea with a
   recorded reason is genuinely valuable — it stops the same dead end being
   re-proposed in six months.

5. **Smallest test.** Every surviving idea ends with the cheapest experiment that
   would produce real information. An idea with no next step is entertainment.

## Output

Two or three at most. **Three excellent ideas a month beat thirty adequate
ones** — volume converts a real capability into a feed he scrolls past.

For each: the connection made, why it is *his* specifically, the strongest
objection, the smallest test.

## Also

Re-run parked ideas against current capability. Ideas that died on timing rather
than merit come back when the mastery record says he can now build the part that
was missing.
