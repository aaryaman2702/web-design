# Concealment fails when the user controls inspection

From the scroll-film playbook: dissolves and crossfades over a bad junction are
forbidden. *"The scrub lets the user park on the seam, which exposes the mask
instantly. Fix the join, don't hide it."*

## Why it generalises

The specific claim is about video seams. The underlying one is broader and more
useful: **a concealment technique only works if the viewer cannot stop and
look.** Cinema can hide a bad cut because playback runs at a fixed rate. A
scroll-scrubbed page cannot, because the pace belongs to the user.

Any medium that hands the user control over inspection speed — scroll, zoom,
pause, inspect element, read the source — invalidates concealment as a strategy
and leaves only correctness.

This connects to a recorded observation about Aaryaman: he *"treats chrome as
debt"* and dislikes technique that announces itself. Same family of judgment,
arrived at independently — which is mild corroboration, not proof.
