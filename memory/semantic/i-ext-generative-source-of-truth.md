# The artifact is source of truth; the container adapts

Stated in the scroll-film playbook as the "footage-first law": design the camera
arc first, then build the page around whatever footage actually comes back.
Never storyboard the site and force the footage to match.

## Why it generalises

The reasoning given is that generated output *drifts* — you cannot fully specify
what comes back — while the surrounding structure is cheap to move. Planning
against an imagined artifact and then forcing reality to match it means paying
regeneration costs to satisfy a plan that was never load-bearing.

That holds anywhere a generative step sits inside a pipeline, not just video.
The inversion of normal practice is the interesting part: conventional process
says specify precisely, then execute. With stochastic output the cheaper order
is execute, observe, then arrange around what you actually got.

## Confidence

`medium` — the reasoning is sound and internally consistent, but this is a
third-party assertion, not something observed working here. It rises if it
survives contact with real work.
