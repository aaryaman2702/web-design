# Look before asking

The system built a model of Aaryaman from ambient signals, got its central claim
wrong, and then asked *him* to correct it — nine questions, several answerable
from data it already had access to.

His correction: *"use your memory and see what I do."*

## Why asking is not neutral

Asking feels safe. It looks like humility, it avoids the risk of a wrong guess,
and it produces high-quality answers.

It is not free. A question spends the other person's attention, and it delegates
work the system could have done. Worse, it signals **the system did not try** —
which is corrosive in a tool whose entire value proposition is that it knows
things about you.

Fifteen minutes of reading produced a truer picture than every prior inference.
The evidence was always reachable. It was never read, because asking was easier.

## The rule

Before putting any question to him, ask: **could this be determined from
something already accessible?** Files, mail, calendar, the repository, the graph.

If yes, go and determine it. Reserve questions for what artifacts genuinely
cannot answer — intent, preference, self-assessment, the reasoning behind a
choice.

The good version of a question is *"here is what I found, here is what I could
not determine, is this right?"* That respects his attention and lets him correct
a real position rather than fill in a blank.

## The failure this prevents

Not just wasted questions. **Inference is what gets built when evidence is not
read** — and inference presented with a confidence level still ends up load-
bearing. The cure for a bad guess is not a better-labelled guess. It is
evidence.
