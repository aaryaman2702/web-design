# Rejected the 3D bottle — "gimmicky", "looked really bad"

**The first real craft taste judgment in the system.** Everything before this
was about how he thinks. This is about what he makes.

He built the Break of Dawn site in two parallel versions — one with an
interactive 3D bottle, one without. **He shipped without it.**

> "without the 3d bottle, it felt gimmicky and it looked really bad but now
> that we have higgsfield it'll be easier to create beautiful animations"

*Parse: the version shipped was the one without the bottle; "gimmicky" and
"looked really bad" describe the 3D bottle version, answering the "why". If that
inverts the intended meaning, one word corrects it and this node gets rewritten.*

## Two separate rejections, and separating them is the whole value

**1. It announced itself.** "Gimmicky" is a precise word. It does not mean ugly
— it means the technique was visible as technique. A 3D product model on a brand
site is impressive *as a feature* and reads as a trick *as an experience*. The
visitor notices the 3D before they notice the product.

This lands exactly on a principle already in the profile: **he treats chrome as
debt — every element earns its presence or gets cut.** The 3D bottle was the
most expensive element on the page and it did not earn its place.

**2. Execution fell short.** "Looked really bad" is a separate complaint, and
conflating the two would produce badly wrong future work.

The critical evidence for that separation is the second half of the sentence:
*"now that we have higgsfield it'll be easier to create beautiful animations."*

**He is not anti-spectacle. He is anti-unconvincing.** He killed the ambitious
version because it did not clear his quality bar, and is explicitly waiting for
better tooling to attempt the ambition again. That is a completely different
person from one who prefers restraint on principle.

## What generalises

**Ambition is welcome; visible effort is not.** He will reach for the harder,
richer treatment — and cut it without sentiment the moment it reads as a trick
or falls short of convincing.

The bar is not *"is this impressive?"* but *"does this disappear into the
experience?"* A technique that survives is one the visitor feels without
noticing.

Practical consequence for anything built for him: **a half-executed ambitious
idea is worse than a well-executed restrained one.** He has already made that
trade once, on his own work, and paid for it by discarding months of build.

## Dimensions moved

`concept` and `restraint`, with a strong implication for `motion` and
`interaction` that is not yet directly evidenced — the animation work is
prospective, not shipped.

## What this does NOT license

It does not mean "keep things simple." That reading would be a serious
misinterpretation and would produce exactly the timid work he would reject next.
He removed a 3D model and immediately started planning richer animation. The
constraint is on **conviction**, not on ambition.
