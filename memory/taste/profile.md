worse than no profile — it produces confident generic output while claiming
personalisation, and every downstream module inherits the fiction. The engine

## Early observations

Three judgments, all from this session, all about *thinking* rather than visual
craft. Marked low confidence — a single session is one data point wearing the
costume of a pattern.

### Prefers architecture that survives its context — `concept`
Rejected a design that was well-fitted to his current tools, explicitly because
it was fitted to his current tools. Values the version that is still correct in
ten years over the version that is optimal today.

*Generalises to:* he weights durability over immediate fit. Expect him to prefer
the more general solution when the cost is bounded — and to be irritated by
cleverness that only works right now.

### Wants the reasoning, not just the artifact — `copy`
Repeatedly pushed past *what* toward *how it thinks*. Deliverables that assert
without showing their basis will read as shallow to him.

*Generalises to:* explain mechanism. "It works" is not persuasive; "it works
because" is.

### Rejects accommodation — `concept`
Asked, unprompted, for a system that challenges rather than agrees. Ambition is
oriented toward becoming more capable, not toward feeling supported.

*Generalises to:* agreement is worth nothing to him. Disagreement with evidence
is the higher-status contribution.

## How to fill this fast

The bottleneck is evidence, and evidence is cheap to generate deliberately:

- Show him three options and record which he picks — and, more usefully, *why*
  he rejected the other two. Rejections carry more information than selections.
- Ask what he has built that he is proud of. Nothing models taste faster than
  the work someone defends.
- Ask what he finds overrated. Negative taste is sharper, more specific, and
  more predictive than positive taste.
- Record every correction with the reason, not the fix.

Ten real judgments make this useful. Fifty make it predictive.
