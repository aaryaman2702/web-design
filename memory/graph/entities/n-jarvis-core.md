# JARVIS

The system itself, as a node in its own graph.

This is not decoration. JARVIS is a real artifact Aaryaman is building, it is
evidence in the engineering mastery domain, and it will be the subject of
decisions, reflections, and taste judgments like anything else he makes. A
system that cannot reason about itself cannot improve itself deliberately.

## What it is

A cognitive architecture in five layers ordered by rate of change: identity →
cognition → engines → modules → adapters. Tool-agnostic above the adapter layer,
which is the property that is supposed to let it survive a decade of vendor
churn.

Seven always-on engines — reflection, taste, decision, idea, opportunity,
curiosity, evolution — operating over a typed knowledge graph stored as plain
Markdown.

## Why it exists

To increase the quality of Aaryaman's thinking, decisions, and output over
years. Not to answer prompts faster.

The architecture decision and its full reasoning: `d-2026-0730-01`.

## What would prove it works

Deliberately falsifiable, so this node can be graded rather than admired:

- It says something he had not thought of, that he acts on
- It disagrees with him and turns out to be right — and the disagreement log
  proves it rather than memory claiming it
- Its taste predictions get measurably better
- A vendor gets swapped with a one-line registry edit
- He reaches for it by default rather than by discipline

## What would prove it failed

The honest version, written now so it cannot be rationalised away later:

- It becomes a productivity dashboard — the outcome he explicitly ruled out
- The engines run but nothing in memory ever changes a decision
- Architecture keeps growing while the graph stays empty
- He stops opening it

**The last one is the real test.** A system this elaborate that goes unused is
worth less than a crude one that runs daily, and the elaborateness makes the
failure harder to admit.
